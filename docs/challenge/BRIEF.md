## Problem

Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data concerns exploring crop rotations using NASA Earth observations, local soil information, crop characteristics and farmer priorities. Rotation means a sequence across seasons; a single-crop suggestion or an environmental dashboard alone misses the proposed decision need.

The working interpretation is to help a farmer compare a current plan with alternatives supporting adaptation and soil-health considerations under changing conditions. Water access, planting seasons, markets, equipment and local restrictions matter. The guide's “at least three strategies” language comes from an unverified local-event reproduction; do not label it an official requirement yet.

## Planning interpretation

Inputs: field/location, past crops, season calendar, soil knowledge, irrigation budget, restrictions and priorities. Outputs: comparable rotation timelines, feasibility reasons, water exposure and other explicit trade-offs, provenance, missing information and uncertainty.

NASA observations should materially influence a decision indicator. Soil moisture is not soil fertility; greenness is not measured soil health; precipitation is not irrigation supply. A historical replay is not a forecast.
